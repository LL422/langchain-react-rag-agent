Robotic Vacuum Cleaner - Maintenance Guide

Daily Maintenance Tasks:
1. Empty the dustbin after each cleaning session, or every 2-3 days for light use. A full dustbin reduces suction power by up to 50%.
2. Check the brush roll for visible hair and string tangles. Quick removal of surface tangles prevents deeper wrapping.
3. For mop models: remove the mopping pad and wash it with mild soap and water. Allow it to air dry completely before reattaching.

Weekly Maintenance Tasks:
1. Deep clean the main brush roll: remove it from the robot, use the included cleaning tool or scissors to cut away tightly wound hair, and wipe the brush housing compartment clean.
2. Clean the side brush: remove it from the robot, pull off any wound hair, and check that the bristles are not permanently bent or frayed. Side brushes with bent bristles should be replaced.
3. Wipe all sensors with a dry microfiber cloth: LiDAR turret on top, front infrared sensors, cliff sensors on the bottom (3-6 sensors), and charging contacts.
4. Clean the wheels: check drive wheels and the front caster wheel for hair tangles. The caster wheel is often overlooked but frequently collects hair around its axle.
5. Inspect the filter: tap it against a trash can to remove loose dust. Do NOT wash HEPA filters with water unless the manufacturer specifically says it's washable.

Monthly Maintenance Tasks:
1. Deep clean or replace the filter: if the filter appears grey or clogged even after tapping clean, it's time to replace. Most filters last 1-3 months depending on usage.
2. Check the brush roll for wear: bristles should be straight and somewhat stiff. If they're bent over, worn down, or missing, replace the brush.
3. Clean the dustbin with water (if the manufacturer allows): remove the filter first, wash the dustbin with mild soap and water, and let it dry completely before reinstalling.
4. Inspect the charging dock: clean the contacts, check the power cord for damage, and ensure the dock is still positioned correctly.
5. For mop models: descale the water tank using a manufacturer-recommended descaling solution. Mineral buildup from tap water can clog the water pump and nozzles over time.
6. Check the side brush for wear: bent or frayed bristles reduce edge cleaning effectiveness.

Quarterly Maintenance Tasks:
1. Replace the filter if you haven't already. Even if it doesn't look severely dirty, filter efficiency degrades over time.
2. Check the battery health: if you notice significantly reduced runtime (more than 20% decrease from original), the battery may be approaching the end of its life.
3. Inspect and clean the self-emptying dock (if applicable): check the dust bag, clean the suction channel, and wipe the dock's sensors.
4. Check for firmware updates: manufacturers regularly release updates that improve navigation, add features, and fix bugs.
5. Deep clean all accessible parts: remove and clean the brush guard, check for debris inside the brush compartment, and clean all crevices with compressed air or a soft brush.

Annual Maintenance Tasks:
1. Consider replacing the battery if it's 2-3 years old, even if it still works. Battery degradation is gradual, and a new battery restores original runtime.
2. Replace the main brush roll if it's over a year old and shows visible wear. Worn brushes reduce cleaning effectiveness by 30-40%.
3. Replace the side brush. These are inexpensive and tend to accumulate micro-damage over time.
4. Check wheel treads for wear. While wheel motors rarely fail, treads can become smooth and reduce traction on certain surfaces.
5. For mop models: replace the water tank if you notice leaks, cracks, or if the pump isn't working efficiently.
6. Perform a factory reset if you're experiencing persistent software issues. Re-map your home afterward.

Consumable Replacement Schedule:
- HEPA Filter: every 1-3 months (depending on usage and home environment)
- Main Brush Roll: every 6-12 months
- Side Brushes: every 3-6 months
- Dust Bags (self-emptying dock): every 30-60 days
- Mopping Pads: every 3-6 months
- Battery: every 2-3 years
- Wheels: as needed (usually 3-5 years)

Signs Your Robot Needs Immediate Maintenance:
1. Unusual noise during operation (grinding, squeaking, rattling)
2. Visible debris trail left behind after cleaning
3. Significantly reduced battery runtime
4. Robot frequently getting stuck in areas it previously navigated easily
5. Decreased suction power noticeable on carpets
6. Dustbin not filling up despite the robot running a full cycle
7. Water not dispensing properly from the mop tank
8. Error messages appearing frequently in the app
9. Robot taking longer than usual to complete cleaning
10. Odor from the robot or dustbin

Cleaning Solution Recommendations:
1. For cleaning the dustbin: use mild dish soap and warm water. Rinse thoroughly and dry completely.
2. For cleaning sensors: use only a dry microfiber cloth. Do not use water, alcohol, or cleaning solutions on sensors.
3. For mopping pads: use mild laundry detergent. Avoid fabric softener as it can reduce the pad's water absorption.
4. For the robot body: wipe with a slightly damp microfiber cloth. Do not spray water directly on the robot.
5. For descaling the water tank: use manufacturer-recommended solutions or a diluted white vinegar solution (1:3 vinegar to water ratio), then rinse thoroughly.

Things to NEVER Do:
1. Never immerse the robot in water or spray water directly on it
2. Never wash HEPA filters with water unless the manufacturer explicitly states they are washable
3. Never use bleach, ammonia, or harsh chemicals to clean any part of the robot
4. Never use abrasive scrubbers that can scratch sensors or the robot body
5. Never operate the robot without a filter installed
6. Never use cleaning solutions in the water tank unless approved by the manufacturer
7. Never attempt to open the sealed battery compartment or motor housing
8. Never use compressed air at high pressure directly on sensors - it can force dust deeper inside
9. Never place the robot or dock near heat sources (radiators, heating vents, direct sunlight)
10. Never use the robot to clean up wet spills, paint, chemicals, or construction debris

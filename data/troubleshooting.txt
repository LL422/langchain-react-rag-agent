Robotic Vacuum Cleaner - Troubleshooting Guide

Issue 1: Robot Won't Turn On
Possible causes and solutions: (1) Battery is completely drained - place the robot on the charging dock and wait at least 30 minutes before attempting to power on. (2) Power switch is off - check if your model has a physical power switch (usually on the side or underside) and ensure it's in the ON position. (3) Battery connection is loose - this requires opening the battery compartment and reseating the battery connector. Contact support if under warranty. (4) Charging dock has no power - verify the dock's power adapter is connected and the indicator light is on. Try a different outlet.

Issue 2: Robot Won't Charge
Troubleshooting steps: (1) Clean the charging contacts on both the robot and the dock with a dry microfiber cloth. Dirty contacts are the most common cause of charging issues. (2) Ensure the dock is placed on a hard, flat surface against a wall with at least 0.5m clear on each side and 1.5m in front. (3) Check if the dock's indicator light is on - if not, test the power adapter with another outlet. (4) The battery may be too hot - if the robot just finished cleaning and the battery is warm, let it cool for 30 minutes before charging. (5) Battery may be deeply discharged - leave on the dock for 2-4 hours. (6) If none of these work, the battery may need replacement.

Issue 3: Robot Gets Stuck Frequently
Common causes and fixes: (1) Loose cables and cords - the most common cause of stuck robots. Tidy all cables before cleaning sessions. Consider using cable management solutions. (2) Rug tassels and fringe - tuck fringe under the rug or use rug tape. Some robots allow setting no-go zones over rugs. (3) High thresholds between rooms - check the robot's maximum climbing height specification. You may need threshold ramps. (4) Narrow spaces - the robot may enter tight spaces between furniture legs and be unable to turn around. Use virtual walls or no-go zones to block these areas. (5) Dark-colored floors or carpets - can trigger cliff sensors, causing the robot to avoid or stop on these surfaces. Try covering cliff sensors with reflective tape (consult manufacturer first). (6) Loose items on floor - shoes, socks, toys, pet bowls. Tidy the floor before scheduled cleaning.

Issue 4: Poor Suction Performance
Diagnostic steps: (1) Empty the dustbin - a full dustbin dramatically reduces suction. (2) Clean the filter - a clogged filter is the most common cause. Tap it clean or replace if it's been over 2 months. (3) Check the brush roll - hair and debris wrapped around the brush prevent proper agitation. Remove and clean thoroughly. (4) Check for blockages in the suction channel - debris can clog the pathway between the brush and dustbin. Remove the dustbin and check the inlet. (5) Verify the robot is in the correct suction mode - check the app to ensure it's not in Eco/Silent mode. (6) Clean the side brush - tangled side brushes don't sweep debris effectively toward the main inlet.

Issue 5: Robot Not Connecting to App or Wi-Fi
Step-by-step troubleshooting: (1) Confirm your Wi-Fi network is 2.4GHz - nearly all robot vacuums only support 2.4GHz, not 5GHz. (2) Move the robot within 2-3 meters of the router during setup. (3) Disable any VPN on your phone during pairing. (4) Verify the Wi-Fi password is entered correctly, avoiding special characters that may cause issues. (5) Restart the router and the robot by turning both off for 30 seconds. (6) Ensure MAC address filtering is disabled on your router. (7) Try a different smartphone for initial setup. (8) Check the manufacturer's server status - some brands require cloud connectivity. (9) If using Android, ensure location services are enabled during setup (required for Wi-Fi scanning).

Issue 6: Robot Not Returning to Dock
Why this happens and how to fix: (1) Dock placement - the dock needs 0.5m clear on each side and 1.5m in front. No reflective surfaces (mirrors, windows) within 1m. No direct sunlight hitting the dock's sensor. (2) Dirty dock sensors - clean the infrared sensors on both the robot and dock. (3) Obstacles between robot and dock - the robot needs a clear path back. Close doors or clear major obstacles. (4) Robot lost its map - this can happen if the robot is moved manually to a different location. Place the robot within 2m of the dock and it should re-localize. (5) Low battery during long return - if the battery is critically low before reaching the dock, the robot will stop. Pick it up and place it on the dock manually. (6) Software glitch - restart the robot and try again.

Issue 7: Robot Cleaning in Random Patterns Instead of Systematic Rows
Possible causes: (1) Map is being rebuilt - after a reset or moving to a new location, the robot may use random navigation until it completes mapping. This is normal for the first 1-3 cleaning sessions in a new environment. (2) Navigation sensor issue - LiDAR sensor may be dirty or obstructed. Clean the LiDAR turret on top of the robot with a dry cloth. (3) Camera obstructed - on camera-based robots, ensure the lens is clean and unobstructed. (4) Dark environment - camera-based robots need some light. Turn on lights or use a LiDAR model. (5) Furniture was moved between sessions - the robot needs to re-map changed areas.

Issue 8: Battery Draining Too Quickly
Diagnostic approach: (1) Check cleaning mode - Max/Turbo mode can reduce runtime by 40-50% compared to Eco mode. Switch to a lower power setting if appropriate. (2) Carpet boost - cleaning mostly carpet reduces runtime. This is expected behavior. (3) Battery age - lithium-ion batteries degrade over 2-3 years. If runtime was previously good and has gradually declined, the battery likely needs replacement. (4) Check for obstructions - wheels or brushes with tangled hair increase motor load and drain battery faster. Clean thoroughly. (5) Temperature - very hot or cold environments affect battery performance. Keep the robot at room temperature. (6) First few cycles - new robots may have shorter initial runtimes until the battery is properly conditioned after 3-5 full charge/discharge cycles.

Issue 9: Robot Making Unusual Noises
Noise diagnosis: (1) Rattling sound - likely debris caught in the brush roll or side brush. Remove and clean. (2) Grinding noise - check for hard objects (small stones, screws, broken plastic) in the brush compartment or suction channel. (3) Squeaking noise - wheels or brush bearings may need lubrication (check manufacturer guidance first) or replacement. (4) Loud whining - the vacuum motor may be working harder due to a clogged filter or full dustbin. Empty and clean. (5) Clicking noise - side brush hitting furniture repeatedly, or debris stuck in wheel mechanism. (6) Beeping and voice alerts - these are normal notifications. Check the app for the specific alert meaning.

Issue 10: Robot Leaving Debris Behind
Reasons and solutions: (1) Dustbin is full - empty it and the performance should return to normal. (2) Filter is clogged - this is the most common cause of reduced pickup. Clean or replace. (3) Brush roll is worn - bristles that are bent or worn can't effectively agitate debris from carpet. Replace the brush. (4) Side brush is bent or tangled - a damaged side brush won't sweep debris effectively. Replace if bent beyond repair. (5) Wrong suction mode - increase suction power for the affected area. (6) Large debris - robots can only handle debris up to a certain size (typically about 1cm). Pick up large items manually. (7) The robot didn't cover that area - check the cleaning map in the app to verify coverage. The robot may have missed the area due to an obstacle or navigation issue.

Issue 11: Mopping Function Not Working Properly
Troubleshooting mopping issues: (1) No water coming out - check water tank is filled and properly seated; ensure the water pump isn't clogged (try running a descaling cycle with citric acid solution if recommended by the manufacturer); check that mopping mode is enabled in the app. (2) Uneven mopping - the mop pad may not be attached evenly; the floor may be too uneven; the pad may be too dry (increase water flow in app settings). (3) Streaks on floor - the mop pad may be too dirty; too much water flow; cleaning solution residue. Wash the mop pad and adjust water flow. (4) Robot refusing to mop - check that the water tank is detected (some models have sensors); verify the mop pad attachment is correct; ensure the robot firmware is updated. (5) Water leaking - the tank seal may be damaged; the tank may be overfilled; the water inlet cap may not be fully tightened.

Issue 12: Robot Falling Down Stairs Despite Cliff Sensors
This is a safety-critical issue. Steps: (1) Clean all cliff sensors on the bottom of the robot with a dry cloth. Dirty sensors are the most common cause. There are typically 3-6 sensors around the perimeter. (2) Check for sensor damage - if any sensor appears cracked or damaged, stop using the robot on upper floors immediately and contact support. (3) Dark or reflective flooring - very dark floors or highly polished surfaces can confuse infrared cliff sensors. Some manufacturers offer reflective tape solutions. (4) Direct sunlight - strong sunlight hitting cliff sensors can overwhelm them. Close curtains or run the robot at different times. (5) Sensor calibration - some models allow recalibration via the app. (6) If the problem persists, do not use the robot near stairs until it has been serviced.

Issue 13: Robot Not Cleaning on Schedule
Troubleshooting: (1) Verify the schedule is correctly set in the app, including the correct time and days. (2) Check that the robot is on the dock and charging. It won't start if not docked. (3) Ensure Do Not Disturb (DND) mode isn't conflicting with the schedule. DND typically prevents cleaning during set quiet hours. (4) Check that the robot is connected to Wi-Fi - schedules are typically cloud-based and require connectivity. (5) Verify the robot's internal clock is correct (usually synced from the internet). (6) Power outages may have reset the robot. Re-check schedule settings after an outage. (7) Firmware update may have reset schedules. Reconfigure after updates.

Issue 14: Dustbin Full Indicator Always On
Possible causes: (1) The dustbin is actually full - empty it. (2) The sensors inside the dustbin compartment are dirty or obstructed - clean the interior sensors with a dry cloth or compressed air. (3) Dust or debris is blocking the full-bin sensor's optical path - these are usually infrared beam sensors on either side of the dustbin inlet. (4) The filter is extremely clogged, causing backpressure that the sensor interprets as a full bin. Replace the filter. (5) Software glitch - restart the robot.

Issue 15: Robot Ignores Virtual Walls or No-Go Zones
Why this happens: (1) Map corruption - the saved map may be corrupted. Delete and re-map the floor. (2) No-go zone was set after mapping changes - if furniture was moved, the zone may no longer align correctly. Re-map. (3) Firmware issue - update the robot to the latest firmware. (4) Sensor obstruction - if the robot can't localize itself on the map, it won't respect virtual boundaries. Clean all sensors. (5) The virtual wall is too small - very small no-go zones may be ignored. Make them slightly larger. (6) For physical virtual wall beacons: check batteries, ensure they're positioned correctly, and that the signal isn't blocked.

Issue 16: Water Tank Leaking
Diagnostic steps: (1) Check the rubber seal on the tank cap - if cracked, deformed, or missing, water will leak. Replace the seal or the entire tank if needed. (2) Check for cracks in the tank body - even hairline cracks can cause slow leaks. Inspect the tank under good light. (3) Overfilling - most tanks have a MAX fill line. Overfilling can cause leaks through the cap or vent. (4) Improper seating - the tank may not be fully inserted into the robot, causing the water outlet to not seal properly. Remove and re-insert firmly. (5) Water inlet valve issue - the one-way valve that releases water may be stuck open. Contact support for repair. (6) If the leak is from the robot itself (not the tank), the internal water system may have a damaged tube or pump. This requires professional repair.

Issue 17: Robot Not Finding Its Way Back to a Specific Room
How to address: (1) The map may need updating - if furniture was moved, re-map the house. (2) Room labels may be incorrect - check in the app that rooms are correctly named and separated. (3) The path to the room is blocked - a closed door, moved furniture, or new obstacle is blocking access. (4) Room is too small for the robot to recognize as distinct - very small rooms (under 3 sqm) may be merged with adjacent areas. Merge small rooms in the app settings. (5) Robot may have trouble with the specific route (long corridors with many turns). Try placing the robot closer to the target room.

Issue 18: Error Codes and What They Mean
General guide (check your specific model's manual for exact codes): Error 1/2: Wheel issue - check for obstructions, clean wheels. Error 3/4: Brush issue - clean main brush and side brush. Error 5: Dustbin not detected - re-insert the dustbin properly. Error 6: Cliff sensor issue - clean sensors, move robot away from stairs/dark surfaces. Error 7/8: Bumper/LiDAR issue - clean sensors, remove obstructions. Error 9: Battery issue - check charging contacts, battery temperature. Error 10: Network/Wi-Fi error - check connection. Error 11/12: Fan/motor issue - clean filter, check for blockages. Error 13: Water tank/mop issue - check tank installation. Most errors resolve with cleaning the relevant sensors or components. If errors persist, contact support.

Issue 19: App Shows Robot as Offline
Troubleshooting: (1) Verify the robot is powered on and on the charging dock. (2) Check your phone's internet connection - try toggling between Wi-Fi and mobile data. (3) Force-close and reopen the app. (4) Check if the robot's Wi-Fi indicator light is on. (5) Restart the robot. (6) Check if your router is online and working properly. (7) The manufacturer's cloud servers may be experiencing issues - check their status page or social media. (8) Try logging out and back into the app. (9) Ensure the robot hasn't been reset to factory settings. (10) Check if your router recently changed - a new router requires re-pairing the robot.

Issue 20: Robot Spins in Circles or Moves Erratically
Causes and fixes: (1) Dirty or obstructed wheel sensors - clean both drive wheels and remove any tangled hair or debris. (2) Stuck bumper - the front bumper may be jammed. Gently press and release it multiple times to check for smooth movement. Clean around the bumper edges. (3) LiDAR malfunction - if the LiDAR turret isn't spinning, the robot can't navigate. Listen for the turret spinning sound. Clean the LiDAR housing. (4) Firmware corruption - perform a factory reset and re-configure. (5) Cluttered environment - too many obstacles close together can confuse navigation. Remove some obstacles or clean a different area. (6) Wheel motor failure - if one wheel is not working, the robot will spin. This requires repair.

Issue 21: Self-Emptying Dock Not Emptying the Robot
Troubleshooting: (1) Check that the dock's dust bag isn't full - a full bag prevents airflow. Replace if needed. (2) Check for blockages in the dock's suction channel - debris can clog the path between the robot and the dock's dust bag. Remove and check the channel. (3) Ensure the robot is properly docked and aligned with the dock's suction port. (4) The dock's motor may have failed - listen for whether the dock's motor runs when emptying is triggered. If not, the dock needs repair. (5) Check the rubber seals on the robot's dustbin outlet and the dock's inlet - damaged seals reduce suction efficiency. (6) Verify the auto-empty setting is enabled in the app. Some models allow disabling this feature.

Issue 22: Robot Not Detecting Carpets
Possible causes: (1) The carpet detection sensor (usually an ultrasonic or pressure sensor on the underside) is dirty. Clean the sensor. (2) The carpet is too thin - very low-pile carpets or flat-weave rugs may not trigger the sensor. (3) The carpet color is too dark - some sensors struggle with very dark surfaces. (4) Carpet boost feature is disabled in the app - check settings. (5) Firmware issue - update to the latest firmware. (6) The robot may need to be on the carpet for several seconds before detection activates. This is normal for some models.
